Privacy Policy

Last updated 17 August 2026

reminal is built so that there is very little to collect. This page describes the little there is, and (where possible) how to verify each claim yourself rather than take it on trust.

This website

reminal.app sets no cookies, uses no analytics, and loads no third-party scripts, fonts, or tracking pixels. There is no advertising network, no session recording, and no fingerprinting. You can confirm this by opening your browser's developer tools: the only network requests the page makes to anything other than reminal.app are to Loops, and only at the moment you submit the waitlist form.

The site is served by Cloudflare, which acts as our hosting provider. Like any host, Cloudflare processes the connection data required to deliver a web page (including your IP address) under its own policies. We do not receive, store, or query that data.

The page measures your round-trip time to the nearest Cloudflare edge by requesting /ping, an endpoint that returns an empty response and stores nothing. The resulting number is displayed to you and never leaves your browser.

The waitlist

If, and only if, you submit the waitlist form, we receive:

  • your email address;
  • whether you selected "just me" or "my team";
  • if you selected "my team", the size range you chose.

That's the whole record. We use it for one purpose: to email you when the hosted version is available, and to understand whether the people waiting are individuals or companies, which affects how it gets built and priced. We do not sell it, rent it, or share it for anyone else's marketing.

This data is stored by Loops (loops.so), our email provider, acting as a processor on our behalf. Every email includes an unsubscribe link. To have your record deleted outright, email the address at the bottom of this page and it will be removed.

The software

The reminal client and relay contain no telemetry or analytics of any kind. No usage reporting, no crash reporting, no third-party SDK. This is a claim you can check, and the checks are published in the security architecture:

grep -rIn "analytics\|telemetry\|posthog\|sentry\|mixpanel" internal/ cmd/   # no output
grep -rn  "console\." cloudflare/src                                        # no output

Two things do involve a network connection, and you should know about both:

  • Update checks. At most once every 24 hours the client asks GitHub for the latest release and the relay for a minimum-version floor. GitHub sees your IP address and user agent, as it would for any download.
  • Sessions. Your terminal and window data is encrypted end to end with AES-256-GCM. The relay routes ciphertext it cannot read, and holds routing metadata for no more than ten minutes after an agent disconnects. Session content is never stored.

One deliberate exception: reminal expose forwards a local port to a public URL for visitors who hold no reminal key, so that traffic is not end-to-end encrypted and is readable by the relay in transit. It is PIN-gated by default. If that matters for what you are exposing, self-host the relay.

There are no others. No analytics vendor, no error-reporting service, no CRM, no payment processor, no advertising network. The fuller version of this table lives in docs/security/subprocessors.md.

Your choices

  • Self-host the relay and no third party is in your session path at all. It runs on Cloudflare's free tier; the instructions are in the repository.
  • Don't submit the form and we hold nothing about you whatsoever.
  • Ask for deletion. Email us and your waitlist record is removed. You don't need to give a reason.

Depending on where you live you may have statutory rights to access, correct, export, or erase personal data we hold. Given that the only thing we hold is a waitlist record, exercising any of them is a single email.

Children

reminal is a developer tool and is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their personal data.

Changes
